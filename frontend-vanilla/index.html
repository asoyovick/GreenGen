How the platform works

1. Select an investigation

Choose a predefined scientific question, geographical area, and historical period supported by the dataset.

2. Analyze observations

The backend retrieves NASA observations and the scientific engine computes grid-level trends, magnitude, significance, and uncertainty.

3. Explore evidence

Inspect maps, grid cells, time series, and reports. Non-significant results are not presented as stability, and limitations remain visible.

Scientific methodology overview

What we report

  • Trend direction and magnitude (e.g. Sen’s slope) with units
  • Statistical evidence (e.g. Mann–Kendall, with documented assumptions)
  • Uncertainty/confidence intervals and data completeness
  • Grid-level classifications: significant increase/decrease, no significant trend detected, insufficient data, or inconclusive

Scientific integrity

  • No fabricated observations or results; synthetic fixtures are labelled and never shown as real findings
  • Statistical significance, magnitude, and practical importance are distinguished
  • Missing data is not treated as zero; uncertainty and limitations are disclosed
  • Trends do not imply causation

Data-source attribution

NASA Earth observations. When real analyses are available, results will cite dataset name/version, retrieval details, spatial/temporal coverage, and methodology/provenance as provided by the backend. Current prototype views may use clearly labelled precomputed/synthetic demo layers when live investigation services are unavailable.
